Initials

jī (machine)
Tongue flat, air flows through — "jee" with no English "j" sound
q — qī
qī (seven)
Like "ch" in cheese — sharp puff of air
x — xī
xī (west)
Like "sh" but with your tongue flat

Spelling rules (test!)

Rule 1: standalone i → written as yi; u → wu; ü → yu
Rule 2: after j q x, the dots on ü disappear — ju, qu, xu (still sound like ü!)
Rule 3: after n l, keep the dots — nǚ, lǜ

4
z · c · s + dividing mark 第四課
dì sì kè · Dental consonants; the apostrophe rule
▼

Initials — z · c · s

z — zì
zì (character)
Like "dz" — no air
c — cí
cí (word)
Like "ts" — strong puff of air
s — sì
sì (four)
Like English "s"

Dividing mark (')

When a syllable starting with a / o / e follows another syllable, add ' to split them clearly:
xīngqí'èr (Tuesday) — not xīngqíèr
Xī'ān (the city) — not Xian
kù'ài (fond of) — not kuài

5
zh · ch · sh · r + 不 tone change 第五課
dì wǔ kè · Retroflex consonants; bù → bú rule
▼

Retroflex initials

zh — zhī
zhī (to know)
Tongue curled back — "j" with retroflex
ch — chī
chī (to eat)
Tongue curled back — "ch" with air
sh — shì
shì (to be)
Tongue curled back — "sh"
r — rì
rì (day/sun)
Like "r" in American "red", tongue curled

The 不 (bù) rule — tested often!

bù (not) stays as bù (4th tone) before T1, T2, T3:
bù chī (don't eat), bù néng (can't), bù hǎo (not good)

bù → bú (2nd tone) when followed by a 4th-tone word:
bú shì (is not), bú kèqi (you're welcome), bú yào (don't want)

6
Word stress + 一 (yī) tone change 第六課
dì liù kè · Natural rhythm; the yī sandhi rule
▼

Word stress tips

🎵 Tone length order: T2 > T1 > T4 > T3
🎵 Two-syllable words — the first syllable is usually stressed:
hóngchá, xuéxiào, diànnǎo, míngtiān
🎵 Neutral-tone 2nd syllable — 1st is strong and long:
māma, bàba, jiějie, dìdi

The 一 (yī) rule

Stays yī (T1) standing alone or at end: shíyī (11), dì yī (first)

Changes to yì (T4) before T1/T2/T3:
yì tiān (one day), yì nián (one year), yì diǎn (a little)

Changes to yí (T2) before T4:
yí piàn (a piece), yídìng (definitely), yí cì (once)

👅 Tongue twisters

Classic 繞口令 (ràokǒulìng) — great for mastering specific sound distinctions.

EASY · s vs sh
四是四,十是十,十四是十四,四十是四十
Sì shì sì, shí shì shí, shísì shì shísì, sìshí shì sìshí.
4 is 4, 10 is 10, 14 is 14, 40 is 40. The classic beginner twister — drills the 四/十 distinction.
EASY · ma tones
媽媽騎馬,馬慢,媽媽罵馬
Māma qí mǎ, mǎ màn, māma mà mǎ.
Mom rides the horse, the horse is slow, mom scolds the horse. Pure tone drill.
MEDIUM · n vs l
牛郎戀劉娘,劉娘念牛郎
Niú láng liàn Liú niáng, Liú niáng niàn Niú láng.
Niulang loves Lady Liu, Lady Liu misses Niulang. Taiwan-friendly: tests your n/l which many Taiwanese merge.
MEDIUM · chi/shi
吃葡萄不吐葡萄皮,不吃葡萄倒吐葡萄皮
Chī pútao bù tǔ pútao pí, bù chī pútao dào tǔ pútao pí.
Eating grapes without spitting the skins, not eating grapes but spitting the skins. The most famous Chinese tongue twister.
HARD · sh vs s
石獅寺前有四十四個石獅子
Shí shī sì qián yǒu sìshísì gè shí shīzi.
In front of Stone Lion Temple there are 44 stone lions. Nightmare for Taiwan speakers who flatten retroflex.
HARD · aspiration
八百標兵奔北坡,炮兵並排北邊跑
Bā bǎi biāo bīng bēn běi pō, pào bīng bìng pái běi biān pǎo.
800 soldiers run up the north slope, artillery troops run side-by-side on the north. Pure b/p aspiration test.
